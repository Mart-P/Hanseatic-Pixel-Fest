Every big adventure starts somewhere

One table.
A thousand worlds.

Our story began with a sketchbook, a handful of favorite games, and a simple thought: what if Hamburg had a weekend where every kind of fan felt at home?

It started, as the best things often do, around a crowded table. A few Hamburg friends had spent years swapping comics, lending each other games, and meeting up after work to make things of their own. They loved the big conventions, but kept wishing for something a little less overwhelming: more time to talk to artists, more chances to actually play, and a friendlier first step for people who were just curious.

One evening, someone opened a notebook and drew a tiny arcade cabinet beside the harbor. The sketch was wonky. The idea was not. What if the city’s independent comic artists, game makers, and fans all had a place to find one another?

“A good convention shouldn’t feel like a crowd you have to get through. It should feel like a table with one more chair pulled up for you.”

So they started small: a room, a few borrowed consoles, a stack of handmade zines, and an invitation to bring a friend. People came to play, stayed to talk, and left with new names in their sketchbooks and new games on their minds. By the end of the day, the little gathering had become something bigger than its organizers had imagined.

That first spirit still shapes the fest. We make room for the first-time visitor as much as the lifelong fan; for the artist showing their first comic as much as the studio with a new game to share. And because Hamburg is part of the story, we wanted to invite the whole world to join in—not just the people who already know the city.
